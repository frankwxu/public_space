\section*{Objective}
This assignment guides you through the commands in the lecture slides. You
will execute each required command, capture its relevant output, explain what
the command and result mean, and document any errors you encounter.

\section*{Instructions}
Use the following format for every submission:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Standard 8.5-by-11-inch pages.
    \item At least 12-point text throughout, including text in figures and
          screenshots.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Step 1: Access the Lab Instructions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Open the
          \href{https://github.com/frankwxu/digital-forensics-lab/tree/main/Basic_Computer_Skills_for_Forensics}{lecture slides and lab resources in the GitHub repository}.
    \item Download the assigned lecture slides. Figure~\ref{fig:lab-structure}
          shows the download process.

          \begin{figure}[ht]
              \centering
              \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{images/download_ppt.jpg}
              \caption{Steps for downloading the lecture slides}
              \label{fig:lab-structure}
          \end{figure}

    \item Review the slides and identify the required tasks and their order.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Step 2: Complete the Assigned Tasks}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Complete the tasks in the order shown in the lecture slides.
    \item Execute each required command and verify its result.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Step 3: Capture Screenshots}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Capture the relevant output of each required command.
    \item Crop each screenshot to the relevant command and output. Remove
          unnecessary blank space and errors unrelated to the task.
    \item Use screenshots with a resolution at least as high as the example
          in Figure~\ref{fig:lab-command}.
    \item In the submitted document, all text visible in a screenshot must
          appear at least as large as 12-point text.
    \item Label each screenshot with its corresponding task or step number.
    \item You may use Snipaste (\url{https://www.snipaste.com/}) or the
          Microsoft Snipping Tool to capture and crop screenshots.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Step 4: Explain Your Work}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item For each command, explain its purpose, options or arguments, and
          result. Use at least 12-point text.
    \item Follow this example:

          \textbf{Command:} \texttt{ll *.txt}

          \textbf{Description:}
          In the \texttt{\textasciitilde/readme} directory,
          \texttt{ll} displays a long listing when the system defines it as an
          alias for \texttt{ls -l}. The \texttt{*.txt} wildcard limits the
          results to names ending in \texttt{.txt}. Each row shows the file's
          permissions, link count, owner, group, size, modification date and
          time, and name.

          \begin{figure}[H]
              \centering
              \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{images/command_explain.png}
              \caption{A sample screenshot of the command \texttt{ll *.txt}}
              \label{fig:lab-command}
          \end{figure}

          \textbf{Result:}
          The output in Figure~\ref{fig:lab-command} lists the two matching
          text files, \texttt{openwebui.txt} and \texttt{run\_LLM.txt}. The
          leading hyphen in each permission string indicates a regular file.
          Both files are owned by user \texttt{frank} and group
          \texttt{frank}; the remaining columns show their sizes and last
          modification dates and times.

\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Step 5: Handle Errors}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Record errors that prevent you from completing an assigned task.
    \item Include a cropped screenshot showing the relevant error message.
    \item Briefly describe the error and the troubleshooting steps you tried.
    \item Errors caused by operating-system or software-version differences
          will not affect your grade when they are documented as directed.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Grading Criteria}
Each lab is worth 10 points unless otherwise announced. A lab without a
hands-on component will use an alternative 10-point problem-solving assignment.
\textbf{Note:} Screenshots and command descriptions in the lecture slides are
teaching examples and may not satisfy the criteria below. Follow this document
when preparing your submission.

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Completion of all tasks (50\%)}
          \begin{itemize}[nosep]
              \item Missing required tasks or steps.
              \item Missing required commands or results.
              \item Evidence that does not demonstrate successful completion.
          \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Accuracy of explanations (30\%)}
          \begin{itemize}[nosep]
              \item Missing explanations.
              \item Inaccurate or vague explanations. For example,
                    ``\texttt{ll *.txt} shows files'' is too vague because it
                    does not explain the long-listing format or the wildcard.
                    ``\texttt{ll *.txt} lists every file in the directory'' is
                    inaccurate because the command lists only names ending in
                    \texttt{.txt}.
              \item Repeating the command without explaining its purpose,
                    options, or result.
          \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Quality of screenshots and documentation (20\%)}
          \begin{itemize}[nosep]
              \item Visible text that appears smaller than 12 points, including
                    text in figures and screenshots.
              \item Figures or screenshots with lower resolution than the
                    example in these instructions.
              \item Screenshots containing commands, output, or error messages
                    unrelated to the assigned task.
              \item Unnecessary blank space or screenshots not cropped to the
                    relevant command and output.
          \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
